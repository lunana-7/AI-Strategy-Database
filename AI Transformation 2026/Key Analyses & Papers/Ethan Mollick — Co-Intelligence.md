# Ethan Mollick — Co-Intelligence: Living and Working with AI

> Wharton professor Ethan Mollick's *Co-Intelligence* (2024) has become the defining framework for **human-AI collaboration** in enterprise settings — a NYT bestseller that bridges academic rigor with practical guidance.

---

## About the Author

**Ethan Mollick** is a professor at the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania, specializing in innovation, entrepreneurship, and the effects of AI on work and education. His Substack "One Useful Thing" has become one of the most influential AI commentary platforms, with 800K+ subscribers. He is widely regarded as one of the most practical voices in the AI discourse — neither a doomer nor an uncritical booster.

---

## Core Thesis

Mollick argues that AI should be understood not as a tool, but as a **co-intelligence** — an entity that works alongside humans in ways that are fundamentally different from previous technologies. The key insight is that AI doesn't automate tasks in the traditional sense; it **changes the nature of the tasks themselves**.

His central framework proposes four principles for working with AI:

### The Four Rules of Co-Intelligence

1. **Always invite AI to the table** — Use AI in every task, even where you don't think it will help, to discover unexpected applications
2. **Be the human in the loop** — AI augments but doesn't replace human judgment; the human provides context, values, and oversight
3. **Treat AI like a person (but not a human)** — Anthropomorphizing AI slightly produces better interactions than treating it purely as software
4. **Assume this is the worst AI you'll ever use** — Current capabilities are the floor, not the ceiling; design workflows for improvement

---

## Key Arguments

### The Jagged Frontier
Mollick's most influential concept: AI capabilities form a **"jagged frontier"** — spectacular at some tasks, mediocre at others, with no intuitive boundary between the two. This means:

- You cannot predict where AI will excel without **experimentation**
- Expert intuition about AI capabilities is often **wrong**
- Organizations must adopt a **discovery mindset** rather than a planning mindset

### The End of Average Performance
AI disproportionately helps **lower performers** while providing less benefit to experts. This has profound implications:

- The **performance distribution** within organizations narrows dramatically
- Traditional performance management systems may become obsolete
- The value of expertise shifts from **execution** to **judgment and taste**

### The Homework Problem
Using education as a microcosm, Mollick examines how AI disrupts knowledge work:

- Traditional assessments become meaningless when AI can produce B+ work
- The valuable skills shift to **asking the right questions** and **evaluating outputs**
- Organizations need to redesign work processes, not just add AI to existing ones

### Organizational Implications
- **Middle management** faces the most disruption — routine coordination becomes automatable
- Companies need **AI-native workflows**, not AI-augmented legacy processes
- The biggest risk is **not using AI**, not using it poorly
